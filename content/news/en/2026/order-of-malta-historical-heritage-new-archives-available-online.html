Important new Order of Malta historical heritage documents are now accessible online through the Magistral Archives portal. The latest update, curated by the Conservatoria Magistrale, significantly expands the documentary collection available to scholars, researchers and history enthusiasts, making available new archival sections of particular value for reconstructing the Order’s history.

Among the main new developments is the publication of the first section of the historical archive of the Grand Priory of Lombardy and Venice, kept at the Castello headquarters in Venice. This marks an important milestone in the recovery, digitization and enhancement project launched a year ago, also achieved thanks to the collaboration with the Hill Museum & Manuscript Library (HMML), engaged in the systematic digitization of documents.

The material now freely available includes the proceedings of the Priory Assemblies and Chancelleries, dating from 1395 to 1828, and the processes of admission of Venetian and Lombard knights, dated between the end of the 16th and the middle of the 19th century. The latter are preserved both in individual fascicules and in the miscellaneous volumes called Diversorum. Numerous instruments for consulting the archive have also been made available, including twelve indexes of the Priory of Venice (1546-1792), two volumes of the Priory of Lombardy (1735 and 1787-1788) and ten volumes of the unified Grand Priory, relating to the period between the beginning of the 19th century and 1937.

Another important innovation concerns the opening for full consultation of the archive of Grand Master Ferdinand von Hompesch (1749-1805), comprising over 400 archival units. The documents offer testimony to a crucial phase in the Order’s history, marked by the loss of Malta following the French occupation of 1798, the exile of the Grand Master and his subsequent political activity.

Added to this documentation are the documents relating to the so-called “Russian Period”, produced by the section of the Order established in St. Petersburg under the protection of Tsar Paul I. Broken down into three subseries, the collection includes documents relating to the activity of the Order’s governing bodies, relations with southern Italy, the admission of knights and the administration of commanderies, as well as correspondence between representatives of the Russian court, Grand Master Tommasi and Lieutenants Guevara Suardo, Di Giovanni and Busca.

The publication of these new collections represents a further step in preserving and enhancing the Order of Malta’s historical memory, promoting access to documentary sources of great interest for historic research.
