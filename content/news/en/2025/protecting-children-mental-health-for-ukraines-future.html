It is not only buildings that need rebuilding in Ukraine, but also future generations that must be saved. On the second day of the Ukraine Recovery Conference (URC2025) in Rome, Pavlo Titko, Head of Malteser Relief Service of Ukraine—the Order of Malta’s relief corps in the country—spoke in the panel dedicated to inclusion and social cohesion.

The session was opened by Alessandra Locatelli, Italian Minister for Disabilities, and Iryna Postolovska, Ukraine’s Deputy Minister of Social Policy for European Integration. They are about to sign a Memorandum of Understanding for joint initiatives aimed at supporting adolescents, the social and work reintegration of veterans, and the protection of people with disabilities during crises and emergencies.

The Head of Malteser Ukraine described the psychological consequences of the conflict on children, and the contribution of the Order of Malta—together with all its entities—in addressing the issue. According to national data, 75% of Ukrainian children show symptoms of post-traumatic stress, such as anxiety, depression, aggression, isolation, silence. One in five suffers from nightmares or sleep disorders; one in ten rejects school or social interaction. “Investing in their mental health,” Titko emphasized, “does not only mean helping them survive the war, but also helps the whole society to heal and recover.”

From the start of 2025 the Ukrainian government launched a new psycho-social assistance package, featuring 124 specialized centres, over 5,000 territorial outpatient clinics, and multidisciplinary mobile teams. Within this framework, the Order of Malta has developed specific programs to provide individual, family, and group psychological support in various centres across the country. Through Malteser Ukraine, more than 60,000 consultations have been delivered in three years, benefitting over 37,000 adults and about 45,000 children and minors, both among internally displaced persons and refugees in neighbouring countries.
